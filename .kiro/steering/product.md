# Product: Vixir — open-source virtual practical laboratory

## Problem
Many schools in Nigeria and across Africa cannot afford fully equipped labs. Students watch one demonstration, then sit practical exams (WAEC, NECO, university first-year) where they must set up apparatus, take readings, tabulate, plot and calculate on their own.

## Product
A browser-based lab (installable PWA) for physics, chemistry, biology, electronics and engineering. Students:
1. read a briefing and pass a safety / readiness check,
2. set up apparatus on a virtual bench,
3. take readings from instruments that behave like real ones (resolution, zero error, parallax, human reaction time),
4. tabulate, plot and calculate,
5. get a report marked step by step against their OWN data.

Teachers assign practicals to classes and see class-wide results and common mistakes.
Vix is a voice-and-vision lab guide students can call at any time; it explains the screen and asks guiding questions.

## Principles (apply to every feature)
- Simulate the PRACTICAL, not just the phenomenon. Imperfect readings are a feature.
- Every student gets unique, reproducible data (seeded), so copying is pointless and marking is fair.
- Real-world transfer: apparatus must look and behave like the real thing so students recognise it in a real lab.
- Works on a cheap Android phone on weak internet; offline where possible.
- Never give answers in graded mode (applies to Vix, hints and UI copy).
- Free and open source (MIT). No paywalls, no "pricing" anywhere in the product.

## Users
- Student (13–22): secondary school SS1–SS3, polytechnic, university year 1.
- Teacher / lecturer: assigns, reviews, comments.
- Institution admin: manages people and classes.
